Question: How does the transformer architecture avoid the vanishing gradient problem?

--- TOP 2 ANSWERS ---
0.702 -> Transformer models employ residual connections (also known as skip connections) that add the input of a layer to its output. This creates direct pathways for gradients to bypass multiple layers, ensuring that gradients can flow more easily to earlier layers and preventing them from diminishing to insignificance, which is a key factor in avoiding vanishing gradients.

0.655 -> The transformer architecture avoids the vanishing gradient problem through its core mechanism of self-attention, which allows it to directly model dependencies between any two positions in the input sequence regardless of their distance. This direct connection, unlike the sequential processing in RNNs, provides shorter paths for gradients to flow during backpropagation, thus mitigating the vanishing gradient issue.

0.526 -> Layer normalization, applied within transformer blocks, normalizes the activations within each layer. This helps to stabilize the training process by keeping the mean and variance of activations consistent, which in turn prevents extreme values that could lead to vanishing or exploding gradients, thereby contributing to the model's ability to handle long-range dependencies without gradient issues.
0.520 -> The positional encoding mechanism in transformers, by explicitly injecting information about the relative or absolute position of tokens, ensures that even distant tokens can have their positional information propagated through the network. This prevents the loss of sequential information that can contribute to vanishing gradients in traditional sequential models when dealing with long sequences.
0.452 -> The multi-head attention mechanism, a fundamental component of transformers, allows the model to attend to different parts of the input sequence simultaneously and from different representational subspaces. This parallel processing of information and attention across multiple "heads" helps distribute the gradient flow, making it less prone to vanishing compared to a single, deep, sequential path.
